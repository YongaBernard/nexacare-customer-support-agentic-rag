# AI Governance & Responsible AI Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the governance, safety, security, privacy, and responsible AI requirements for the Customer Support Agentic RAG System.

The objective is to ensure that the AI system operates in a controlled, transparent, secure, and accountable manner.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Customer interactions with the AI support assistant
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) workflows
- Company knowledge-base documents
- AI-generated responses
- Agent decision-making and routing
- Human escalation workflows
- System logs and audit records
- AI evaluation and monitoring processes

---

## 3. AI Risk Principles

The system shall follow these principles:

### Accuracy
AI responses should be grounded in approved company knowledge sources.

### Transparency
The system should make it clear when a response is generated by AI.

### Human Oversight
High-risk, sensitive, uncertain, or unresolved customer cases must be escalated to a human support agent.

### Privacy
Personally identifiable information (PII) must be protected and should not be unnecessarily processed or exposed.

### Security
Secrets, API keys, credentials, and sensitive configuration must never be committed to source control.

### Accountability
AI interactions and important system decisions should be auditable through appropriate logging.

### Fairness
The system must not intentionally discriminate against customers based on protected or irrelevant characteristics.

---

## 4. Knowledge Governance

The AI assistant may retrieve information only from approved knowledge sources.

Knowledge-base documents should:

- Have an identifiable owner
- Have a defined purpose
- Be reviewed periodically
- Be version controlled
- Be removed or updated when obsolete
- Be classified according to sensitivity

Unapproved documents must not be used as authoritative sources.

---

## 5. Hallucination Controls

The system should minimize unsupported AI-generated claims.

The assistant should:

1. Retrieve relevant evidence before answering factual questions.
2. Prefer approved company documentation over model knowledge.
3. Avoid inventing policies, prices, refunds, guarantees, or commitments.
4. Clearly communicate uncertainty when sufficient evidence is unavailable.
5. Escalate cases when the system cannot confidently answer.

---

## 6. Human Escalation

The system must support human escalation when:

- The customer requests a human agent.
- The issue involves a complaint or dispute.
- The customer reports a safety-related issue.
- The system detects high urgency.
- The system cannot retrieve sufficient supporting information.
- The customer requests an action outside the AI's authority.
- The system detects potentially sensitive information.

The AI must not make decisions outside its approved authority.

---

## 7. Data Privacy

The system should follow data minimization principles.

Only information necessary to provide customer support should be processed.

Sensitive information should not be stored unnecessarily.

API keys, credentials, and environment variables must be stored securely and excluded from source control.

---

## 8. Access Control

Access to system components should follow least-privilege principles.

Users and services should only receive the permissions required to perform their tasks.

Administrative functions should not be exposed through the customer-facing interface.

---

## 9. Auditability

Important system events should be auditable, including where appropriate:

- Customer request
- Detected intent
- Sentiment classification
- Urgency classification
- Retrieved knowledge sources
- AI response
- Escalation decision
- System errors
- Human handoff

Logs must not unnecessarily expose sensitive customer information.

---

## 10. AI Evaluation

The system should be evaluated for:

- Retrieval accuracy
- Response relevance
- Groundedness
- Hallucination rate
- Escalation accuracy
- Safety
- Robustness
- Response latency

Evaluation results should be documented and used to improve the system.

---

## 11. Monitoring

The production system should monitor:

- Error rates
- Retrieval failures
- AI response quality
- Escalation frequency
- Latency
- Abnormal usage patterns
- Safety-related events

Significant failures should trigger investigation and remediation.

---

## 12. Change Management

Changes to:

- AI models
- Prompts
- RAG configuration
- Knowledge sources
- Agent workflows
- Safety controls
- Governance policies

should be tested before being deployed to production.

Material changes should be documented.

---

## 13. Accountability

The system owner is responsible for:

- Maintaining governance controls
- Reviewing system risks
- Monitoring AI performance
- Maintaining approved knowledge sources
- Reviewing incidents
- Ensuring appropriate human oversight

AI-generated output must not be treated as automatically authoritative.

---

## 14. Policy Review

This policy should be reviewed periodically and whenever significant changes are made to the AI system, its data sources, models, architecture, or intended use.
